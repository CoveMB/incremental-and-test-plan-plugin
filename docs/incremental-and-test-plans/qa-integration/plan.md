### QA-design output contract

`qa-plan.md` is a readable planning document, not a machine schema or authority receipt. It records:

1. Selected source(s), intended outcomes/journeys, exclusions, inspected baseline and relevant existing test/tooling/evidence inventory. Plan-only future interfaces and commands stay provisional. Existing tests are inspected for assertions and applicability; their mere presence is not passing evidence.
2. Material failures, their consequences, reversibility and evidence gaps. Clarify consequential ambiguity with the user rather than inventing business rules, expected answers, legal conclusions, or requirements.
3. A compact design table: source outcome/journey or risk; useful existing coverage/gap; proposed check with independently justified expected behavior; necessary environment/data and isolation; owner/timing and required result evidence. Omit irrelevant empty categories.
4. Bounded implementation work for missing evidence: owning code/test/CI areas, dependencies, fixtures, meaningful assertions, target-supported commands where actually established, and acceptance. Reuse current useful coverage, helpers, native capabilities and installed tools before recommending additions. Material tooling/cost/environment choices remain decisions; no installation is implied.
5. Important untested conditions, expert/human obligations, current review readiness, and the preparation-only next action. Preserve applicable source/target gates and the existing Gate 2/Gate 3 semantics. Integrated QA and increment artifacts form one review candidate; do not require duplicate sets of reviews merely because there are two documents.
6. When warranted by the certification assessment below, a separate certification work item with its subject, evidence, dependencies, prerequisite runbook, action owners, closure and renewal. Embed it in this document; a second mandatory artifact, registry or execution task is unnecessary. An assessment finding no separate certification need can be one short justified statement.

Choose techniques according to the actual behavior and consequence: unit, integration, contract, end-to-end, regression, property-based, fuzz, targeted mutation, security, performance, accessibility, visual, exploratory, human acceptance, specialist, migration, recovery and operational checks. Given/When/Then or Gherkin is an optional way to express an acceptance story when useful; do not mandate a BDD framework. Coverage and quality metrics are supporting diagnostic signals, never proof of correctness or substitutes for assertions. Do not produce a row for every test technique or force expensive techniques on low-risk documentary work.

For important claims, establish the expected result from approved requirements, trusted examples or a justified reference independent of the implementation under test. Legacy parity may demonstrate preservation, but not correctness of a deliberately changed rule. Identify material wrong behavior the check should detect; plan a failing regression demonstration or targeted mutation only when warranted. Review weakened assertions, broadened tolerances, skipped tests, removed cases and excessive mocking against the approved contract. Separate agents and green suites can share assumptions; retain independent review and explicit uncertainty.

For critical behavior crossing increments, designate the combined journey's owning increment/global obligation, relevant combined candidate/environment and required evidence. Component passes cannot establish an unexercised integration property. A software rollback cannot be assumed to reverse data disclosure, financial effects or other irreversible consequences. When release is in scope, retain the actual responsible human decision, specialist evidence and operational/recovery obligations. Implementation acceptance is not release readiness, certification, or legal assurance.

### Conditional certification planning

Assess target policy first, then material assurance gaps: external/provider behavior, persistent data, privileges/security, supported installation/upgrade/removal, recovery, or outcomes local checks cannot establish. These are reasons to assess, not automatic demands for a live environment or another task. Keep ordinary tests with their feature; separate certification work is appropriate for a distinct assurance boundary or an explicit source obligation. A low-risk document edit may need no certification. Here “certification” means the target's evidence-backed capability acceptance; never imply accredited, regulatory or legal certification from a test pass. Identify qualified authorities/processes separately where required.

When warranted, design an independently reviewable certification card/work item after its actual implementation prerequisite. Use the target's existing subject identifiers, registry/evidence conventions and required gates when present; otherwise ordinary Markdown is sufficient. No universal capability registry, digest schema, every-feature rule, new approval system or mandatory live-provider matrix. A new optional proposal cannot become a mandatory dependency/closure gate without the user's material decision or applicable project policy. Source-required certification remains required even with supplementary QA design excluded; capture it directly in the incremental plan when no QA document is produced.

The certification design must identify:

- **Subject and applicability:** exact capability/feature, version or candidate, relevant behavior contract and configuration/dependency boundary, supported baseline and environment. Reuse the project's subject/digest convention if it has one; provisional plan-only identities must be resolved at concrete preparation. Bind actual evidence to this subject and actual prerequisite correspondence, never to a name or green label alone.
- **Evidence and closure:** appropriate local, end-to-end, lifecycle, protected-staging/provider, persistence/security, success/failure, cleanup and recovery outcomes; justified independently expected results and causal subject-specific assertions. Identify what each check can establish, required human/expert decisions, and which dependency/phase/release closures it controls under actual policy. Shared journeys may cover several subjects only with separate causal assertions; implementation acceptance and one subject's success do not certify another subject or a composite capability.
- **Prerequisite runbook:** at concrete certification preparation, derive dated, current official setup instructions or evidence that no human setup is required. Where applicable name account/resource owners and tiers, least-privilege roles, credential names/scopes/lifetime/approved secret store without values, domains/callbacks/allowlists, protected non-production targets, synthetic identities/data, preflight, bounded polling/timeouts/quotas/spend and evidence retention. Include step-by-step cleanup/revocation, rollback and recovery; identify each human/automation action owner and approval checkpoint. Unknown setup is pending, not a no-setup conclusion. Early plans may name unresolved prerequisites and owners; execution readiness requires the concrete runbook, without freezing provider details months ahead.
- **Reuse and renewal:** retain valid unaffected evidence and historical acceptance; reopen affected readiness when the subject, material contract, environment/deployment boundary, relevant dependencies, supported lifecycle edge or recovery behavior changes. Describe missing outcomes and their owner. Neither a moved HEAD nor age alone requires every expensive check to rerun; changed applicability cannot inherit old certification by association.

Certification planning only produces documents and preparation handoffs. It does not execute tests, use credentials/providers, deploy, spend, send messages, mutate persistent data, clean up resources/evidence, create actual tasks/chats or set a real certification registry to pending/certified. Plan approval, implementation acceptance and ordinary gate bypasses supply none of those separate action permissions. Reuse the existing review/gate policy; required certification remains visibly pending until actual applicable evidence and authorized decisions are available.

Preserve the current evidence capture/retention policy. Hold proof for active certification consumers until the named event; optional storage disposition alone does not block demonstrated product scope. Source-required certification cleanup/recovery is an acceptance obligation and remains unresolved until actually evidenced or explicitly amended. The terminal retention review is a separate closure task, not a substitute for that proof or authority to delete.

### Combined flow and exclusion semantics

- **Creation:** QA design is on unless the user explicitly excludes it. Inspect the selected sources and relevant project context, create/reconcile `qa-plan.md`, and incorporate its in-scope checks/owners into feature increments and warranted separate certification cards. The QA document proposes verification; it cannot silently amend product requirements or source-required gates.
- **Exclusion:** a natural-language instruction such as “exclude supplementary QA design” omits that extra design/document, not meaningful feature tests, review scopes, specialist evidence, source/target gates, required integration verification or required certification/runbook work. Record the exclusion concisely. Do not introduce a CLI parser or configuration flag solely for this choice.
- **Standalone:** `qa-design` writes one requested `qa-plan.md`, with optional verbatim `source.md` for pasted input and scoped review notes. It does not manufacture an incremental plan. Its implementation work and copy-ready handoff are sections of the same document.
- **Combined outputs:** the incremental workflow may write `plan.md`, `handoff.md`, `qa-plan.md`, optional verbatim `source.md`, and scoped review notes. These are a single authorized artifact set. Reuse a selected current QA document when its sources/candidate still apply; do not generate a duplicate plan to satisfy the default.
- **Continuation/refinement:** preserve accepted history and human annotations. Reconcile the recorded QA choice, source changes, actual evidence and affected pending work. A new request to change QA mode authorizes only the corresponding planning revision, not past-approval reconstruction, new product behavior or gate waivers. Missing mandatory proof remains missing even with supplementary design excluded.
- **Changing on to excluded:** do not delete an existing QA artifact or source-required obligations. Preserve historical content and clearly identify what remains applicable and what supplementary proposal is no longer governing. No compatibility adapter, legacy schema reader or migration mode is required.
- **Ambiguity/failure:** unclear source/target/outcome, stale or contradictory evidence, material design choices, destination collisions, escaping symlinks or conflicting concurrent edits block affected claims/writes. Keep a useful pending draft where safe; do not silently omit QA, fabricate a pass or overwrite another person's work.
